# Delivery Operations Standard Operating Procedure
**Document type:** Internal Standard Operating Procedure
**Version:** 3.0
**Effective date:** 1 January 2024
**Owner:** Head of Delivery Operations
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the standard process for receiving, processing, and completing deliveries — from pickup at origin to final delivery confirmation — and specifies how each stage is handled, recorded, and communicated.

---

## 2. Scope

Applies to all domestic deliveries within Saudi Arabia: business-to-business (B2B) commercial deliveries, last-mile consumer deliveries, and cross-border GCC shipments.

---

## 3. Delivery lifecycle — standard flow

### Stage 1: Shipment acceptance

**Trigger:** Client submits a delivery order through the portal or directly to operations.

**Actions:**
- Operations assigns a unique shipment reference number (format: WSL-YYYYMMDD-XXXX)
- Driver or warehouse team collects the shipment from the client's location or client drops at hub
- Physical condition of goods is checked and photographed at acceptance
- Weight, dimensions, and item count are verified against the client's manifest
- Any discrepancy at acceptance is noted on the collection receipt and communicated to the client within **2 hours**
- Shipment entered into the tracking system; client receives automated confirmation with tracking link

**Documents required at acceptance:**
- Delivery order / consignment note
- Packing list
- For regulated goods: valid approval documents (SFDA, MOH, SASO)
- For international shipments: commercial invoice, certificate of origin, bill of lading

---

### Stage 2: Hub processing

**Trigger:** Shipment arrives at the operations hub.

**Actions:**
- Shipment scanned into the system upon arrival at hub; timestamp recorded
- Sorted by destination zone and delivery route
- For cold-chain shipments: temperature checked and recorded; placed in appropriate storage (-18°C or 2–8°C)
- Loaded onto delivery vehicle for the appropriate route within the same working day if received before 12:00 noon; next working day if received after 12:00 noon

**Key timing rule:** Goods received by 12:00 noon go out same day. This is the primary metric in the vendor SLA scorecard.

---

### Stage 3: Customs clearance (international shipments only)

**Trigger:** Shipment originates outside Saudi Arabia.

**Actions:**
- Customs coordination team submits declaration on FASAH **within 24 hours of receiving complete documents**
- Pre-clearance submitted at least 24 hours before vessel/aircraft arrival where possible
- Clearance status monitored daily; client notified of any holds within 1 hour of ZATCA notification
- Upon clearance: goods transferred to domestic delivery flow (Stage 2)
- All customs documents archived electronically for 5 years

Refer to the **Customs Clearance Procedure** document for full detail on documentation requirements, hold resolution, and ZATCA rules.

---

### Stage 4: Last-mile delivery

**Trigger:** Shipment loaded on vehicle; driver departs hub.

**Actions:**
- Driver carries the delivery note and contacts consignee 1 hour before estimated arrival
- Delivery attempted within the agreed delivery window
- Consignee signs the proof of delivery (POD); driver photographs signed POD
- POD uploaded to the system within **4 hours of delivery** (target: same day)
- Tracking system updated to "Delivered" status immediately upon completion

**For B2B deliveries:**
- Delivery to the consignee's registered address during business hours (8:00 AM – 5:00 PM, Sunday–Thursday)
- Authorized receiver's name and signature required on POD
- If receiver is not available and the consignee has authorized delivery to a security desk or reception, this is acceptable with a note on the POD

**For consumer / last-mile deliveries:**
- Delivery window communicated to consignee the day before
- Driver calls consignee 30 minutes before arrival
- If no answer, driver waits 10 minutes at the address; photographs the location; returns shipment to hub

---

### Stage 5: Failed delivery handling

**Trigger:** Delivery attempt is unsuccessful.

**Failure reasons and immediate actions:**

| Reason | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| Consignee not available | Photograph location; log attempt; notify client within 2 hours |
| Address incorrect or not found | Contact client immediately; await corrected address |
| Consignee refused delivery | Photograph; document reason; notify client within 1 hour |
| Access denied (gated building, security) | Log; notify client within 2 hours with access instructions requested |
| Goods do not match consignee expectation | Do not leave goods; photograph; notify client; return to hub |

**Re-delivery process:**
- First failed attempt: client notified; re-delivery scheduled within 24 hours at no additional charge
- Second failed attempt: client notified; re-delivery scheduled; no additional charge
- Third failed attempt: client notified; shipment returned to hub; return-to-origin fee applies; client must arrange collection or new delivery instruction within 48 hours

---

### Stage 6: Proof of delivery and closure

**Trigger:** Successful delivery completed.

**Actions:**
- Signed POD uploaded to portal within 4 hours of delivery
- Tracking system updated to "Delivered — POD received"
- Client notified via portal or email with POD confirmation
- Invoice generated for the completed shipment within 24 hours

**For damaged or short deliveries at POD:**
- Consignee notes the discrepancy on the POD before signing
- Driver photographs the damage / shortage with the consignee present
- Client and operations manager notified immediately
- Exception logged as Category B (carrier) or investigated further if cause is unclear
- Claims process initiated per the vendor contract terms

---

## 4. Cold-chain delivery — additional requirements

For temperature-sensitive shipments (pharmaceuticals, food, cosmetics requiring 2–8°C or -18°C):

- Temperature log must be maintained throughout transit (datalogger required)
- Any temperature excursion (reading outside the specified range for more than 15 minutes) must be reported to the client immediately regardless of whether the delivery has been completed
- Client has the right to reject goods if a temperature excursion is confirmed; rejected goods are returned to hub at the Service Provider's cost
- Temperature logs are archived with the POD for the shipment

## 5. Key timelines summary

| Action | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Discrepancy at acceptance reported to client | Within 2 hours |
| Customs declaration submitted (FASAH) | Within 24 hours of receiving complete documents |
| Customs hold notification to client | Within 1 hour of ZATCA notification |
| Goods accepted by 12:00 noon dispatched | Same day |
| Driver contact with consignee before arrival | 1 hour before estimated arrival |
| POD uploaded after successful delivery | Within 4 hours (same day target) |
| Failed delivery notification to client | Within 2 hours of failed attempt |
| Invoice generated after delivery | Within 24 hours |

## 6. Prohibited actions

The following are strictly prohibited and may result in disciplinary action or contract termination:

- Marking a shipment as "delivered" before obtaining a signed POD
- Leaving goods at an unattended address without client authorization
- Delaying a hold notification to a client for any reason
- Disposing of or returning goods to origin without client approval
- Opening sealed packages without a supervisor present and documented reason
