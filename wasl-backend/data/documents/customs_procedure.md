# Saudi Arabia Customs Clearance Procedure
**Source:** ZATCA Rules of Customs Procedures (Decision No. 28624, effective 29 December 2023)
**Applicable to:** All imports into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia via land, sea, and air ports

---

## 1. Overview

All goods entering Saudi Arabia are subject to customs clearance under the authority of ZATCA (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority) in accordance with the GCC Common Customs Law and its implementing regulations. Clearance is processed through the FASAH platform — the official Saudi customs clearance digital portal.

Importers may handle clearance directly or appoint a licensed customs broker to act on their behalf.

---

## 2. Required documentation for importing goods

The following documents must be submitted electronically via the FASAH platform for every import shipment:

- **Commercial invoice** — must include the importer's Commercial Registration (CR) number, full description of goods, unit price, total value in the invoice currency, and country of origin
- **Bill of lading** (sea) or **air waybill** (air) or **road consignment note** (land)
- **Certificate of origin** — certifying the country of manufacture; required for all commercial shipments
- **Packing list** — itemized list of contents, quantities, weights, and dimensions per package
- **HS code classification** — the correct Harmonized System code for every product line; misclassification is the most common cause of customs holds
- **SASO product conformity certificate** — required for regulated product categories (electronics, food, chemicals, safety equipment)
- **Import permit or prior approval** — required for restricted goods (pharmaceuticals, food items, chemicals, medical devices); must be obtained from the relevant Saudi authority before shipment

All documents are archived and retained electronically for a minimum of 5 years per GCC Common Customs Law requirements.

---

## 3. Pre-clearance (advance submission)

Saudi customs allows and encourages pre-clearance to reduce port dwell time.

**Requirements for pre-clearance:**
- Goods must have already been shipped from the country of export (not merely booked)
- The customs declaration must be submitted electronically through FASAH **no later than 24 hours before the goods arrive at the Saudi customs port**
- All supporting documents (invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin) must be attached at the time of submission

Failure to submit within the 24-hour window forfeits the pre-clearance benefit and subjects the shipment to standard queue processing.

As of 29 October 2025, advance submission of the manifest and customs declaration is **mandatory** for goods arriving through Saudi sea ports.

---

## 4. Common causes of customs holds

The following are the most frequent causes of shipments being held at Saudi customs:

| Cause | Description |
|---|---|
| **HS code mismatch** | The HS code on the declaration does not match the product description or SASO classification |
| **Missing CR number** | The importer's Commercial Registration number is absent or incorrect on the commercial invoice |
| **Incomplete certificate of origin** | Missing, expired, or issued by an unrecognized authority |
| **Missing prior approval** | Regulated goods arriving without the required import permit from SFDA, MOH, or other authority |
| **Invoice discrepancy** | Declared value differs significantly from market value; may trigger price verification |
| **Document not in Arabic or English** | ZATCA may require certified translation |

**Typical resolution time:** Documentation holds are resolved in 2–5 working days once the correct documents are submitted through FASAH. Inspection holds may take 5–10 working days depending on the type of goods and the port.

---

## 5. Inspection rules and procedures

ZATCA inspects goods based on risk assessment criteria. Not all shipments are physically inspected.

- Goods may be inspected at the customs office or, for special, perishable, or oversized goods, at a designated off-site location — in which case the **owner of the goods is responsible for transporting them to the inspection site**
- The owner may request a pre-declaration inspection and sampling before submitting the formal customs declaration; upon ZATCA approval, a temporary customs declaration is generated and customs duties are assessed on any samples retained
- Containers must be securely sealed and may only be opened with ZATCA authorization

---

## 6. Customs duties and payment

- **Standard customs duties** are calculated as a percentage of the CIF (Cost + Insurance + Freight) value of the goods
- **Payment deferral:** The importer may request ZATCA to postpone payment of customs duties for up to **30 days** from the clearance date, subject to providing a cash, bank, or documentary guarantee
- **Commercial samples exemption:** Goods with a total customs value not exceeding **SAR 5,000** may be exempted from customs duties upon request by the owner
- **Guarantees:** Goods may be transported to another location within KSA prior to payment of customs duties by submitting a guarantee; the guarantee is released upon payment

---

## 7. Land border crossing procedures

Goods received through Saudi land customs ports (including GCC border crossings) may be redirected to another customs office within KSA for inspection — for example if the shipment requires specialized inspection equipment available at a different port. This redirection does not constitute a hold but will extend clearance time.

A single consignment under one transportation document may be split only in two cases:
- Goods were received incompletely from the exporting country
- Goods' size or weight requires physical separation

---

## 8. Notification of customs hold to shipper/consignee

When a shipment is placed on hold, ZATCA notifies the customs broker (or direct importer) through the FASAH platform. The notification includes:

- The hold reason (documentation / inspection / compliance / valuation)
- The required corrective action
- The deadline for response (typically 5 working days)

If the importer fails to respond within the deadline, the goods may be moved to a bonded warehouse at the importer's cost.

---

## 9. Re-export of non-conforming goods

If ZATCA determines that imported goods do not comply with Saudi regulations and approves re-export, the importer may claim a refund of any customs duties already paid. The re-export declaration must be linked to the original import declaration.

---

## 10. Key contacts and platform

- **FASAH platform:** fasah.gov.sa — all declarations, permits, and clearance status
- **ZATCA helpline:** 19993
- **Customs broker authorization:** managed through FASAH; brokers must be registered and licensed with ZATCA
