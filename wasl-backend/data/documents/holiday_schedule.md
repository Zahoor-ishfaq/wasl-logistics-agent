# Saudi Arabia 2026 Official Holiday Schedule
**For use by:** Operations, Customs Coordination, Customer Service
**Source:** SAMA (Saudi Central Bank) official holiday calendar; Umm Al-Qura calendar projections
**Important note:** Islamic holiday dates (Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Day of Arafat) are confirmed by the Supreme Judicial Council based on moon sighting, typically 1–2 days before the holiday. Dates below are the best available projections as of July 2026. Always verify through official Saudi government channels before making firm commitments.

---

## 2026 Official Public Holidays

| Holiday | Projected dates | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founding Day** | 22 February 2026 (Sunday) | 1 day | Fixed date. Commemorates establishment of first Saudi state in 1727. |
| **Eid al-Fitr** | 19–22 March 2026 (Thurs–Sun) | 4 days | Marks end of Ramadan. SAMA confirmed: starts 19 March (28 Ramadan 1447H). |
| **Day of Arafat** | 26 May 2026 (Tuesday) | 1 day | 9th of Dhul Hijjah. Most important day of Hajj. Tentative — moon sighting dependent. |
| **Eid al-Adha** | 27–30 May 2026 (Wed–Sat) | 4 days | Tentative. Typically 4 days including Day of Arafat. |
| **Saudi National Day** | 23 September 2026 (Wednesday) | 1 day | Fixed date. |

**Total official holiday days: approximately 14 days**

The standard Saudi workweek runs Sunday to Thursday. Friday and Saturday are the official weekend.

## Impact on logistics and customs operations

### Government and customs offices (ZATCA/FASAH)

All government offices, including ZATCA customs operations, **fully close** during Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. FASAH platform submissions can be made electronically but will not be processed until offices reopen.

| Period | Customs clearance status |
|---|---|
| Founding Day (1 day) | Offices closed; clearance resumes next working day |
| Eid al-Fitr (4 days) | Full closure; no clearance processing |
| Day of Arafat + Eid al-Adha (4 days) | Full closure; no clearance processing |
| National Day (1 day) | Offices closed; clearance resumes next working day |

**Delays during Eid periods are expected and normal — they do not constitute a vendor SLA breach.** All SLA calculations exclude officially declared public holiday days.

### Banking operations

Saudi banks including SAMA follow the same holiday schedule. Bank transfers, customs duty payments, and guarantee processing are unavailable during public holidays.

Customs duty deferral of up to 30 days (per ZATCA rules) can be used to bridge a holiday period where payment would otherwise fall due during a closure.

### Carrier and delivery operations

- Major international carriers (DHL, FedEx, Aramex) maintain skeleton operations during public holidays but with significantly reduced capacity
- Local delivery operations typically reduce to emergency-only during Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha
- Cross-border land shipments (Saudi-UAE, Saudi-Kuwait, Saudi-Jordan) are subject to border authority closures which may differ from Saudi holiday schedule

## Holiday exception handling procedure

When a shipment is delayed due to a public holiday, the following procedure applies:

**1. If delay is anticipated (planned):**
- Operations team flags the shipment in the system at least 48 hours before the holiday period
- Customer is notified proactively with revised ETA (holiday end date + standard clearance time)
- No SLA penalty applies; reason logged as "public holiday closure"

**2. If delay was not anticipated:**
- Operations agent assesses the exception: is it genuinely due to the holiday, or is there another factor?
- If holiday-caused: notify customer within 4 hours of identifying the delay, provide revised ETA
- If another factor exists alongside the holiday: investigate and report both causes separately
- Supervisor review required if delay extends more than 2 days beyond the holiday period end

**3. Post-holiday priority processing:**
- Shipments held during a public holiday are queued for priority clearance on the first working day after the holiday
- FASAH pre-clearance declarations submitted before the holiday are processed first
- Customers with perishable or time-critical goods should be flagged to operations before the holiday period begins

## Ramadan working hours (not a holiday but affects operations)

Ramadan 2026 is expected to run approximately 1–29 March 2026.

During Ramadan:
- Government working hours are reduced to **6 hours per day** (typically 10:00 AM – 2:00 PM and 8:00 PM – 12:00 AM for some offices)
- Customs clearance processing is slower; add 1–2 working days to standard clearance timelines during Ramadan
- Many private sector employees work reduced hours; factor this into vendor communication expectations

## Key planning dates for 2026

Shipment managers should plan around these windows:

| Period | Risk level | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| 15–25 February | **Medium** | Pre-position stock before Founding Day; ensure declarations filed |
| 10 March – 25 March | **Very high** | No new customs submissions after 10 March will clear before Eid al-Fitr. Pre-clear all critical shipments by 10 March. |
| 20 May – 3 June | **Very high** | No new customs submissions after 20 May will clear before Eid al-Adha. Avoid scheduling critical deliveries in this window. |
| 20–25 September | **Low** | 1-day closure only; minor disruption |
